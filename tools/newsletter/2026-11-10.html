JOB FOR THE NEXT 10 DAYS
Check your seeds before the catalogues land

Every year I buy seed I already have. So before the new catalogues tempt you, tip out the tin and sort it.

Anything past its date isn't dead, it's just less reliable. Test it: fold ten seeds into damp kitchen paper, pop it in a sandwich bag somewhere warm, and check after a week or two. If six or more sprout, keep the packet and sow a bit thicker. Fewer than that, buy fresh.

Parsnip seed is the one to watch. It doesn't keep, so buy it new every spring.

PROBLEM SOLVER
Yellow leaves on your sprouts and kale?

Don't panic. It's usually the old bottom leaves finishing, which is normal this time of year. Snap them off and put them on the compost. They hide slugs and can go mouldy, and a bit of air round the stems does the plant good.

IN THE KITCHEN
Kale crisps

Strip the leaves off the stalks, tear them into crisp sized pieces, and dry them properly (wet kale steams, it won't crisp). Rub with a teaspoon of oil and a pinch of salt.

Spread them out on a tray in one layer and bake at 150°C (130°C fan) for 15 to 20 minutes, turning once, until crisp but still green. Eat them the same day.
