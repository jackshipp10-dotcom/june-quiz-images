Hello,

We had our first proper frost this week. Everything white and crunchy at eight, and the sprouts looking like they'd been dusted with icing sugar. They don't mind a bit. A frost or two makes them sweeter, same as parsnips.

It's the wind I worry about this time of year, not the cold. So that's the job this time, and it takes ten minutes.

Here's what I'm doing for the next ten days.

JOB FOR THE NEXT 10 DAYS
Stake your sprouts before the wind does

Sprouts are tall and top heavy by now, and winter winds rock them about. That loosens the roots, the plant starts to lean, and the sprouts come out loose and open instead of tight little buttons. Gardeners call it wind rock.

Go along the row and firm the soil round each stem with your heel. Draw a bit of soil up round the base too. Then knock a stout cane or stake in beside each plant, on the side the wind usually comes from, and tie the stem to it in two places with soft string. Not too tight, the stem still has some growing to do.

Do the same for tall kale and purple sprouting broccoli. And when you pick, start at the bottom of the stem and work up.

PROBLEM SOLVER
Orange spots on your leeks?

That's leek rust, and it's very common after a mild, damp autumn. It looks dreadful but the leeks are perfectly good to eat. Peel off the worst outer leaves when you lift them, and the new growth usually comes through clean once it turns properly cold.

Bin the rusty leaves rather than composting them. Next year give your leeks a bit more room, go easy on the feed (soft, lush growth catches it worst), grow them somewhere different, and look for a variety sold as rust resistant.

IN THE KITCHEN
Buttered leeks in ten minutes

Trim two leeks, slice them into thin rings and wash them in a bowl of cold water, swishing well. The grit sinks and the leeks float. Lift them out with your hands, don't tip them into a colander or you'll pour the grit back over.

Melt a good knob of butter in a pan, add the leeks with the water still clinging to them, a pinch of salt and plenty of pepper. Lid on, low heat, ten minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and sweet. A spoon of cream and a little mustard at the end if you're feeling fancy. Lovely with sausages.
