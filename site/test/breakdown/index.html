Your test breakdown

Every answer explained, with a tip from me for each one.

Your level

Jump to the first one you missed

  1. Question 1

    Which way up do garlic cloves go in?

    Answer: Pointy end up

    The flat end is where the roots grow.

    June’s tip: Push each clove in pointy end up, just below the surface, about 15cm apart. Upside down cloves still try to grow, but they waste energy turning round and give you smaller bulbs.

  2. Question 2

    This little visitor in your leaf pile: friend or foe?

    Answer: Friend

    A hedgehog eats beetles, caterpillars and slugs.

    June’s tip: Leave a corner of leaves and logs over winter and a gap under your fence about 13cm wide so hedgehogs can travel between gardens. Never use slug pellets where they feed.

  3. Question 3

    A ring of crushed eggshells keeps slugs off your lettuce.

    Answer: Myth

    RHS trials found eggshells made no difference.

    June’s tip: In RHS trials, eggshells, copper tape and wool pellets didn’t reduce slug damage. What works better: go out with a torch on damp evenings and pick them off, water in the morning not the evening, and grow plenty so a few nibbles don’t matter.

  4. Question 4

    Dried peas from the supermarket will grow pea shoots.

    Answer: Fact

    Soak them overnight and you’ll be cutting shoots in about two weeks.

    June’s tip: Soak a mugful of dried marrowfat peas overnight, press them into a tray of damp compost, keep it damp on a bright windowsill and snip the shoots at about 10cm. Cut once and they’ll often give you a second crop.

  5. Question 5

    Which courgette is the best size to pick?

    Answer: 11cm

    Pick at 10 to 12cm. They’re tender and the plant keeps cropping.

    June’s tip: Courgettes are best at 10 to 12cm long. Check plants every couple of days in summer, because they double in size fast. Leave one and it becomes a marrow, and the plant slows right down.

  6. Question 6

    Which of these should stay out of the fridge?

    Answer: Tomatoes

    The cold dulls their flavour.

    June’s tip: Keep ripe tomatoes in a bowl at room temperature and eat them within a few days. Green ones ripen in a drawer next to a banana. Kale, leeks and carrots all keep better in the cold.

  7. Question 7

    Why do we pile soil up around potato plants?

    Answer: To keep the potatoes in the dark

    Light turns potatoes green, and green potatoes aren’t safe to eat.

    June’s tip: Earth up when the shoots are about 20cm tall: draw soil up around the stems, leaving the top few leaves showing, and repeat every couple of weeks. In a bag, just top up with compost.

  8. Question 8

    Odd one out?

    Answer: Courgette

    The other three are all one plant family.

    June’s tip: Tomatoes, potatoes, chillies and aubergines are all in the same family, so they share pests and diseases like blight. Courgettes belong with squash, cucumbers and pumpkins.

  9. Question 9

    Rhubarb leaves are fine to eat if you cook them.

    Answer: Myth

    Only the stalks. Never the leaves.

    June’s tip: Cut the leaves off as soon as you pull the stalks and put them on the compost heap. And go easy on a new plant: don’t pick at all in its first year so it can build up strength.

  10. Question 10

    Which courgette flower can you pick to eat without losing a courgette?

    Answer: The male flower, on a thin stalk

    The male, on a thin stalk. The female has a baby courgette behind it.

    June’s tip: Male flowers sit on a thin stalk; females have a tiny courgette behind the petals. Pick a few males in the morning while they’re open, stuff them or fry them in batter, and leave enough to pollinate.

  11. Question 11

    Why plant garlic in autumn, not spring?

    Answer: It needs a spell of cold

    Without the cold, the bulb may not split into cloves.

    June’s tip: Garlic needs a month or two of cold to split into cloves, so October and November is the time. Buy proper seed garlic rather than supermarket bulbs, and you’ll lift it next summer when the leaves yellow.

  12. Question 12

    Your tomatoes have started fruiting. Which feed now?

    Answer: High potash

    Potash feeds the fruit rather than the leaves.

    June’s tip: Once the first fruits have set, give a high potash tomato feed every week to ten days. Too much nitrogen gives you lots of leaf and not much fruit.

  13. Question 13

    Rusty tunnels in your carrots. What did it?

    Answer: Carrot fly

    Its maggots burrow into the roots.

    June’s tip: Cover carrots with fine insect mesh from sowing, sealed at the edges. Carrot fly finds them by smell, so sow thinly to avoid thinning, and if you must thin, do it on a still evening.

  14. Question 14

    Save seed from an F1 tomato and you’ll get the same tomato next year.

    Answer: Myth

    F1 seed doesn’t come true. You never quite know what you’ll get.

    June’s tip: F1 plants are a cross of two parents, so their seed grows into a mixed bag. If you want to save seed, grow open pollinated or heritage varieties, which come true year after year.

  15. Question 15

    In a crop rotation, which goes in the same bed as potatoes?

    Answer: Tomatoes

    Same family, same diseases, so they move round together.

    June’s tip: Rotating stops pests and diseases building up in one spot. A simple four bed plan: potatoes and tomatoes, then peas and beans, then cabbages, then roots and onions, moving each group on a bed every year.
